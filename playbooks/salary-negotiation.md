# Salary, Raises, and Offers Playbook

## 1. Scenario

For offer pay, raises, promotions, bonuses, levels, signing bonuses, equity, review dates, and working conditions. Use evidence of ability, market value, and alternatives to improve total compensation.

## 2. Common situations

- An offer is below expectations, but the user wants to keep the opportunity.
- An employee wants a raise or promotion.
- HR cites limited budget or fixed salary structures.
- The user has another offer but is unsure how to mention it.
- The user wants better base pay, bonus, signing bonus, level, or remote terms.

## 3. Common mistakes

- Saying "it feels low" without evidence.
- Threatening not to join or to resign.
- Discussing monthly salary alone, without total pay, level, or reviews.
- Exaggerating competing offers or experience.
- Drawing legal conclusions on employment contracts, noncompetes, or arbitration.

## 4. Objectives

- Primary: align responsibilities and rewards through better total compensation or key terms.
- Secondary: where base pay is constrained, seek signing bonuses, bonuses, equity, level, review dates, or working conditions.
- Minimum: avoid terms clearly below market and the user's BATNA.

## 5. Key risks

- An aggressive tone damages the opportunity.
- Insufficient evidence makes the request seem emotional.
- Competing offers are questioned.
- Cash focus overlooks level, probation, performance criteria, and development.
- Employment, noncompete, or arbitration issues require professional advice.

## 6. Information to gather

```text
Current offer or compensation structure:
Target pay / total compensation:
Responsibilities and level:
Past results and measurable contributions:
Market references:
Other offers or alternatives:
HR / manager feedback:
Acceptable non-cash terms:
Start / review dates:
Employment contract or noncompete risks:
```

## 7. Power assessment

- Alternatives: other offers, internal roles, ability to wait.
- Time: hiring urgency versus need to start work.
- Information: market data, interview feedback, role scarcity.
- Resources: skills, experience, client resources, scarce abilities.
- Evidence: results, project impact, references, performance records.
- Dependence: how strongly the company needs this person.

## 8. Bargaining assets

- Ability: project results, business impact, scarce skills.
- Market: comparable pay and other offers.
- Time: start date, handover speed, key milestones.
- Structure: base, bonus, sign-on, equity, title, review cycle.
- Risk: noncompete, probation, performance standards, role changes.

## 9. Recommended strategy

1. Express interest, appreciation, and willingness to work together.
2. Support the request with responsibilities, market pay, achievements, and alternatives.
3. Discuss total compensation rather than only base.
4. Give a target range rather than a rigid single figure.
5. If cash is constrained, explore signing bonus, bonus, equity, level, or reviews.

## 10. Avoid

- Threatening HR or a manager.
- Inventing offers.
- Unsupported claims that everyone else pays more.
- Excessive firmness before a written offer.
- Ignoring contract terms, probation, or noncompetes.

## 11. First-round wording

```text
I am interested in the opportunity and the team's direction, and would like to find terms that work for both sides.

Considering the responsibilities, my experience in A/B/C, and comparable roles in the market, I would like to discuss total compensation in the X–Y range.

If base pay is constrained, we could also look at a signing bonus, performance bonus, equity, level, or a post-probation review. I want responsibilities, expectations, and rewards to align.
```

## 12. Common reactions

- "This is our maximum."
- "We cannot break the internal salary structure."
- "Join first; we can adjust after strong performance."
- "Other candidates accept this amount."
- "How much is your other offer?"

## 13. Replies

Budget ceiling:

```text
I understand the budget limit. If base pay cannot move, could we review the overall package: signing bonus, performance bonus, equity, level, or a defined six-month compensation review?
```

Join first, adjust later:

```text
I am comfortable demonstrating value through performance. Could we agree the review timing, measures, and adjustment process in advance so expectations are clear?
```

Other offers:

```text
I do have other opportunities in progress, but would prefer to discuss this role on its responsibilities and fit. My target is X–Y; getting closer to that range would make me more inclined to move forward here.
```

## 14. Confirmation

```text
Thank you for the discussion. To confirm:
1. I remain interested in the role and team.
2. My target total compensation is X–Y, based on scope, experience, and market levels.
3. If base pay is limited, we can discuss signing bonus, bonus, equity, level, or review timing.
4. You will respond by X with adjustment possibilities or alternatives.

Please correct anything I have misunderstood.
```

## 15. Review questions

- Was the refusal about base, total pay, level, or process?
- Were actual budget or approval constraints revealed?
- Are non-cash terms still available?
- Is the user's BATNA strong enough?
- Should the next round seek more, accept, defer, or exit?
